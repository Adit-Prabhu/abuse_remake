## Watching the model player

**Conducted.** After completing the level, the playtester watched about three minutes of the
model player's replay.

**What did they think it was trying to do? Were they right?** The playtester thought the model
was trying to kill the enemies before moving forward. They noticed that it kept shooting at the
hanging enemies even though the shots were not doing anything. This was partly correct, since
the model was attempting to clear threats and progress through the level. However, it seemed to
get stuck repeating the same actions instead of realizing that the hanging enemies could not be
damaged. The playtester found this interesting because they had also initially tried shooting
those enemies but eventually moved on.

**Was anything on the replay panel unclear or misleading?** The playtester could follow the
model's movement and shooting, but it was harder to understand why it kept repeating certain
actions. They were unsure whether the model was deliberately attacking the hanging enemies or
simply failing to recognize that its shots were ineffective. They suggested that showing what
the model was trying to accomplish, or why it selected an action, would make the replay easier
to understand.

## Questions

1. **One thing you (or an agent) would have to guess, and the question that removes the guess.**
   One thing the player had to figure out was which objects could actually be interacted with.
   The lightning symbol looked like it might indicate a hidden room, while hanging enemies
   appeared to be valid shooting targets. Neither assumption worked as expected. The player also
   had trouble understanding when to use the S/Down interaction key.
   Question: "Which objects can be interacted with, and how do I know when to use the S/Down key?"

2. **What was hardest to understand?**
   The player initially struggled with shooting and movement, particularly understanding how to
   aim while navigating the level. However, the bigger difficulty seemed to be figuring out which
   actions worked on which objects. For example, they expected the hanging enemies to take damage
   and thought the lightning symbol might hide something. They also struggled with finding the
   correct route when the path changed from horizontal to vertical.

3. **What would you change first?**
   I would improve how the game communicates its controls and interactions. The player had
   difficulty understanding the shooting mechanics and when to use S/Down. Even though the
   control was already included in the pre-play instructions, it was not necessarily obvious how
   it applied to different objects. I would consider making those instructions easier to
   understand and testing whether another player encounters the same problem. I would not
   immediately change the level layout or make the hanging enemies vulnerable, since I want
   Classic mode to preserve the original game's mechanics.

4. **Fun (1–5), and why?**
   **5/5.** The player said they enjoyed the challenge and that completing the level on Hard made
   the experience more satisfying. They felt that winning after dying multiple times was
   rewarding. Despite complaining about the number of enemies and difficulty during the run, they
   continued playing until they finished the level.

## What I changed or would change

| Finding | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Action-key clarity** | The player had difficulty understanding how to interact with objects, despite S/Down already being included in the controls explanation. I would clarify which objects use the interaction key and make the instructions more noticeable. I would then test this with another player to see whether the confusion happens again. |
| **Shooting and movement** | The player commented that shooting felt weird and initially struggled with navigating while aiming. I would make the distinction between movement, aiming, and firing clearer in the pre-play instructions. I would also like to observe another player before deciding whether the actual controls need to change. |
| **Hanging enemies** | The player tried shooting hanging enemies and suggested making them vulnerable. However, hanging enemies being invulnerable is a mechanic inherited from the original Abuse. I would keep this behavior in Classic mode to preserve the original gameplay. This is still an important usability finding because there is no clear indication that shooting these enemies will not work. It also connects to the model-player results, where the model player repeatedly attacked hanging enemies instead of finding another way to proceed. |
| **Route clarity** | Around 2:30, the player searched left and right when the route continued downward. I would not change the level layout based on this single observation. I want exploration and discovering the correct path to remain part of the game. The player eventually progressed, so this confusion did not permanently prevent completion. I would revisit this only if future players repeatedly get stuck in the same location. |
| **Misleading environmental details** | The player thought the lightning symbol on the wall might represent a secret room and tried shooting it. Although that interpretation was incorrect, I would not immediately remove or change the symbol. I would first observe whether other players make the same assumption. |

### Evaluation limitations

- This was one playtest with one player, so the results cannot establish how most players would
  behave.
- The player completed Classic Level 1 in 9:38 with five deaths and five checkpoint restores, and
  received assistance with the controls around the 1-minute mark. At the planned 8-minute cutoff,
  progress was 71.44%, with three deaths.
- The human and model results are not directly comparable. The human could retry from
  checkpoints after death, while the agent episodes ended at their first death, and the agents
  started at a console (or, in the full-level situation, at the start) with fixed tick and
  decision budgets. Therefore, the human's completion does not by itself prove that humans
  perform better than the agents under equivalent conditions.
